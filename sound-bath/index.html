How it feels

Vibration, not volume.

A singing bowl works because you feel it as much as hear it. Dipsana does the same thing on purpose: the pads pass sound through your cheekbones to the inner ear.

Nothing goes in your ear canal, so there’s no pressure, no seal and no muffled world. You stay aware of the room around you, and the sound settles underneath it.

Bone conduction, simply

Your ears are the long way round.

Hearing happens in the cochlea, a small spiral in your inner ear that turns vibration into signals for the brain. Normally sound has to get there through the ear canal, the eardrum and three tiny bones. Bone conduction takes the shortcut.

  • Nothing in the canalNo seal, no pressure, no buds to keep pushing back in.
  • The room stays audibleOpen ears hear voices, doors and traffic over the top.
  • Felt as well as heardLow tones arrive as a light vibration on the cheekbones.

Hertz, by ear

Every sound is a vibration per second.

Hertz (Hz) counts how many times a sound wave vibrates each second. Low numbers feel slow, deep and physical; high numbers sound bright and light. Drag through the range and listen.

Why a bowl shimmers

Two tones, three hertz apart.

When two notes are only a few hertz apart they drift in and out of step, and you hear a slow pulse at the difference between them. 199 − 196 = three soft swells a second. That gentle wobble is a big part of what makes a singing bowl feel alive, and it’s built into the bowls on this page.

What people come for

Why vibration helps you settle.

  1. Something simple to rest on

    A slow, steady tone gives a busy mind one easy thing to follow. That’s the basic idea behind most sound meditation.

  2. A slower pace

    Long tones that swell and fade invite slower, longer breaths. Pair it with the breath above and you’ll feel the rhythm.

  3. Grounded in the body

    Low frequencies land as a light buzz on the cheekbones, so your attention sits in your body rather than your to-do list.

  4. What the research says so far

    In a 2017 observational study of singing-bowl meditation (Goldsmith et al.), participants reported less tension, anger and fatigue afterwards. It’s early, small-scale work and not a treatment, but it matches what many people feel.
